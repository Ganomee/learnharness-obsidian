# DEMO-STAT-12 - Data quality and communication role-play

> [!warning] Simulation provenance

## Dialogue

### Turn 1: Missing data
**Learner:** How should I understand missing data?
**Teacher:** Missingness can change estimates; its pattern and plausible mechanism should be examined before choosing a method. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P120 - missing-data]]

### Turn 2: Duplicate records
**Learner:** How should I understand duplicate records?
**Teacher:** Duplicate records can inflate counts and distort estimates; deduplication rules should match the unit and data-generation process. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P121 - duplicate-records]]

### Turn 3: Unit of analysis
**Learner:** How should I understand unit of analysis?
**Teacher:** The unit of analysis is the entity represented by each analytic observation and must match the research question. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P122 - unit-of-analysis]]

### Turn 4: Data leakage
**Learner:** How should I understand data leakage?
**Teacher:** Data leakage occurs when information unavailable at prediction time enters model training or evaluation. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P123 - data-leakage]]

### Turn 5: Train-test split
**Learner:** How should I understand train-test split?
**Teacher:** A train-test split evaluates predictions on held-out data, but split design must respect time, groups, and dependence. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P124 - train-test-split]]

### Turn 6: Reproducibility
**Learner:** How should I understand reproducibility?
**Teacher:** Reproducible analysis records data provenance, transformations, software, assumptions, and executable steps. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P125 - reproducibility]]

### Turn 7: Uncertainty statement
**Learner:** How should I understand uncertainty statement?
**Teacher:** A useful uncertainty statement names the estimate, interval or uncertainty measure, method, and key assumptions. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P126 - uncertainty-statement]]

### Turn 8: Denominator transparency
**Learner:** How should I understand denominator transparency?
**Teacher:** A reported rate should state its numerator, denominator, population, time window, and exclusions. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P127 - denominator-transparency]]

### Turn 9: Visualization integrity
**Learner:** How should I understand visualization integrity?
**Teacher:** A truthful chart labels axes and units, uses suitable scales, and avoids visual choices that distort comparisons. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P128 - visualization-integrity]]

### Turn 10: Ethical data use
**Learner:** How should I understand ethical data use?
**Teacher:** Ethical data use considers consent, privacy, access, potential harms, and whether collection and reuse are justified. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P129 - ethical-data-use]]
