# DEMO-STAT-03 - Describing distributions role-play

> [!warning] Simulation provenance

## Dialogue

### Turn 1: Frequency table
**Learner:** How should I understand frequency table?
**Teacher:** A frequency table counts observations in categories or value ranges and makes the distribution inspectable. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P030 - frequency-table]]

### Turn 2: Histogram
**Learner:** How should I understand histogram?
**Teacher:** A histogram displays quantitative values in adjacent bins; bin choices affect the visible shape. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P031 - histogram]]

### Turn 3: Bar chart
**Learner:** How should I understand bar chart?
**Teacher:** A bar chart compares category values with separated bars; bar heights encode counts, proportions, or another stated measure. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P032 - bar-chart]]

### Turn 4: Center
**Learner:** How should I understand center?
**Teacher:** A measure of center summarizes a distribution’s typical or balancing value, but does not describe its full shape. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P033 - center]]

### Turn 5: Mean
**Learner:** How should I understand mean?
**Teacher:** The arithmetic mean is the sum of values divided by their count and is sensitive to extreme observations. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P034 - mean]]

### Turn 6: Median
**Learner:** How should I understand median?
**Teacher:** The median is the middle ordered value, or midpoint of the two middle values, and is resistant to extremes. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P035 - median]]

### Turn 7: Mode
**Learner:** How should I understand mode?
**Teacher:** The mode is a most frequent value or category; a distribution can have multiple modes or no uniquely useful mode. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P036 - mode]]

### Turn 8: Skewness
**Learner:** How should I understand skewness?
**Teacher:** A skewed distribution has an asymmetric tail; the tail direction names the skew, not the location of most observations. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P037 - skewness]]

### Turn 9: Outlier
**Learner:** How should I understand outlier?
**Teacher:** An outlier is unusually distant relative to a stated rule or context and should be investigated rather than automatically removed. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P038 - outlier]]

### Turn 10: Robust summary
**Learner:** How should I understand robust summary?
**Teacher:** A robust summary changes relatively little when a small number of observations are extreme; median and IQR are common examples. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P039 - robust-summary]]
