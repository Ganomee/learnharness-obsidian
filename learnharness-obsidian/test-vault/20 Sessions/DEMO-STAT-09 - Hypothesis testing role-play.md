# DEMO-STAT-09 - Hypothesis testing role-play

## Dialogue

### Turn 1: Null hypothesis
**Learner:** How should I understand null hypothesis?
**Teacher:** The null hypothesis specifies a reference claim or parameter value used to calibrate a test statistic. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P090 - null-hypothesis]]

### Turn 2: Alternative hypothesis
**Learner:** How should I understand alternative hypothesis?
**Teacher:** The alternative hypothesis identifies departures from the null that the test is designed to detect. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P091 - alternative-hypothesis]]

### Turn 3: Test statistic
**Learner:** How should I understand test statistic?
**Teacher:** A test statistic summarizes sample evidence in a scale whose null distribution is known or approximated. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P092 - test-statistic]]

### Turn 4: P-value
**Learner:** How should I understand p-value?
**Teacher:** A p-value is the probability, assuming the null and model, of a test statistic at least as extreme as the observed one. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P093 - p-value]]

### Turn 5: Significance level
**Learner:** How should I understand significance level?
**Teacher:** The significance level alpha is a chosen bound on the long-run Type I error rate under the test’s assumptions. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P094 - significance-level]]

### Turn 6: Type I error
**Learner:** How should I understand type i error?
**Teacher:** A Type I error rejects a true null hypothesis; its probability is controlled approximately by the test level under assumptions. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P095 - type-i-error]]

### Turn 7: Type II error
**Learner:** How should I understand type ii error?
**Teacher:** A Type II error fails to reject a false null; its probability depends on the alternative, design, and sample size. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P096 - type-ii-error]]

### Turn 8: Statistical power
**Learner:** How should I understand statistical power?
**Teacher:** Power is the probability a test rejects the null under a specified alternative and design. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P097 - statistical-power]]

### Turn 9: One-sided test
**Learner:** How should I understand one-sided test?
**Teacher:** A one-sided test places its rejection region in one direction, which must be justified and set before seeing results. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P098 - one-sided-test]]

### Turn 10: Failure to reject
**Learner:** How should I understand failure to reject?
**Teacher:** Failure to reject a null is not proof that it is true; the data may be insufficiently informative. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P099 - failure-to-reject]]
