# DEMO-STAT-08 - Confidence intervals role-play

> [!warning] Simulation provenance

## Dialogue

### Turn 1: Point estimate
**Learner:** How should I understand point estimate?
**Teacher:** A point estimate is one sample-based value used to estimate a population quantity. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P080 - point-estimate]]

### Turn 2: Confidence level
**Learner:** How should I understand confidence level?
**Teacher:** A 95% confidence procedure covers the true parameter in about 95% of repeated samples when its assumptions hold. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P081 - confidence-level]]

### Turn 3: Confidence interval
**Learner:** How should I understand confidence interval?
**Teacher:** A confidence interval is an interval produced by a procedure with a stated long-run coverage property. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P082 - confidence-interval]]

### Turn 4: Margin of error
**Learner:** How should I understand margin of error?
**Teacher:** Margin of error is the half-width of an interval around an estimate under a specified method and confidence level. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P083 - margin-of-error]]

### Turn 5: Critical value
**Learner:** How should I understand critical value?
**Teacher:** A critical value is a quantile of a reference distribution used to set an interval or test threshold. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P084 - critical-value]]

### Turn 6: t distribution
**Learner:** How should I understand t distribution?
**Teacher:** The t distribution accounts for estimating a normal population’s standard deviation and has heavier tails at smaller degrees of freedom. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P085 - t-distribution]]

### Turn 7: Proportion interval
**Learner:** How should I understand proportion interval?
**Teacher:** An interval for a proportion should use a method with adequate performance for its sample size and observed rate. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P086 - proportion-interval]]

### Turn 8: Interval width
**Learner:** How should I understand interval width?
**Teacher:** All else equal, higher confidence and greater variability widen an interval, while larger sample size narrows it. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P087 - interval-width]]

### Turn 9: Practical significance
**Learner:** How should I understand practical significance?
**Teacher:** An estimate can be statistically precise yet too small to matter for a real decision; context sets practical importance. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P088 - practical-significance]]

### Turn 10: Interval interpretation
**Learner:** How should I understand interval interpretation?
**Teacher:** A confidence interval does not assign a 95% probability to a fixed parameter after the interval is computed in the frequentist framework. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P089 - interval-interpretation]]
