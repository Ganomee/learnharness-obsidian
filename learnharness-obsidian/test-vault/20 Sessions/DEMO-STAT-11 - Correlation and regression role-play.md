# DEMO-STAT-11 - Correlation and regression role-play

## Dialogue

### Turn 1: Scatterplot
**Learner:** How should I understand scatterplot?
**Teacher:** A scatterplot displays paired quantitative observations and helps reveal form, direction, clusters, and unusual points. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P110 - scatterplot]]

### Turn 2: Covariance
**Learner:** How should I understand covariance?
**Teacher:** Covariance measures joint linear variation and depends on the units of both variables. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P111 - covariance]]

### Turn 3: Correlation
**Learner:** How should I understand correlation?
**Teacher:** Pearson correlation measures linear association on a scale from -1 to 1 and can be sensitive to outliers. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P112 - correlation]]

### Turn 4: Correlation is not causation
**Learner:** How should I understand correlation is not causation?
**Teacher:** An association alone does not establish causation because confounding, reverse direction, and selection can explain it. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P113 - correlation-is-not-causation]]

### Turn 5: Least squares line
**Learner:** How should I understand least squares line?
**Teacher:** The least squares line minimizes the sum of squared vertical residuals for the observed data. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P114 - least-squares-line]]

### Turn 6: Slope
**Learner:** How should I understand slope?
**Teacher:** In a linear model, slope is the fitted change in mean response per one-unit increase in the predictor. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P115 - slope]]

### Turn 7: Intercept
**Learner:** How should I understand intercept?
**Teacher:** The intercept is the fitted response at predictor zero and may lack practical meaning if zero is outside the data context. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P116 - intercept]]

### Turn 8: Residual
**Learner:** How should I understand residual?
**Teacher:** A residual is observed response minus fitted response and measures vertical prediction error in the sample. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P117 - residual]]

### Turn 9: R-squared
**Learner:** How should I understand r-squared?
**Teacher:** R-squared summarizes the fraction of sample response variation explained by a fitted model under a specified definition; it is not causal proof. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P118 - r-squared]]

### Turn 10: Extrapolation
**Learner:** How should I understand extrapolation?
**Teacher:** Extrapolation predicts beyond the observed predictor range and can fail when the relationship changes outside that range. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P119 - extrapolation]]
