## Dialogue

### Turn 1: Difference in means
**Learner:** How should I understand difference in means?
**Teacher:** A difference in means compares arithmetic averages on the same scale and should be paired with uncertainty and context. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P100 - difference-in-means]]

### Turn 2: Paired data
**Learner:** How should I understand paired data?
**Teacher:** Paired data contain linked measurements; analysis should preserve pairing, often by studying within-pair differences. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P101 - paired-data]]

### Turn 3: Two-sample comparison
**Learner:** How should I understand two-sample comparison?
**Teacher:** A two-sample comparison requires checking design, independence or pairing, distributional conditions, and the target quantity. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P102 - two-sample-comparison]]

### Turn 4: Pooled proportion
**Learner:** How should I understand pooled proportion?
**Teacher:** For disjoint groups measuring the same outcome, the pooled proportion is total successes divided by total trials. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P103 - pooled-proportion]]

### Turn 5: Contingency table
**Learner:** How should I understand contingency table?
**Teacher:** A contingency table displays counts for combinations of categorical-variable values. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P104 - contingency-table]]

### Turn 6: Expected cell count
**Learner:** How should I understand expected cell count?
**Teacher:** An expected contingency-table count is calculated under a specified independence model, not read from observed data. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P105 - expected-cell-count]]

### Turn 7: Chi-square statistic
**Learner:** How should I understand chi-square statistic?
**Teacher:** A chi-square statistic aggregates squared observed-minus-expected differences scaled by expected counts. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P106 - chi-square-statistic]]

### Turn 8: Risk difference
**Learner:** How should I understand risk difference?
**Teacher:** Risk difference subtracts two outcome probabilities and is measured in probability points. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P107 - risk-difference]]

### Turn 9: Risk ratio
**Learner:** How should I understand risk ratio?
**Teacher:** Risk ratio divides one outcome probability by another positive probability and is dimensionless. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P108 - risk-ratio]]

### Turn 10: Simpson’s paradox
**Learner:** How should I understand simpson’s paradox?
**Teacher:** An aggregate association can differ from subgroup associations when group composition differs; inspect relevant stratification and causal structure. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P109 - simpson-s-paradox]]
