# DEMO-STAT-01 - Data and variables role-play

> [!warning] Simulation provenance
> Assistant-authored learner/teacher dialogue for populating a demo graph. It was not generated by an active LearnHarness session, external model, or API. The linked concept notes are reviewable introductory references.

## Dialogue

### Turn 1: Population and sample
**Learner:** How should I understand population and sample?
**Teacher:** A population is the full set of units a question concerns; a sample is the subset actually observed. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P010 - population-and-sample]]

### Turn 2: Observational unit
**Learner:** How should I understand observational unit?
**Teacher:** An observational unit is the person, object, or event on which a measurement is recorded. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P011 - observational-unit]]

### Turn 3: Variable
**Learner:** How should I understand variable?
**Teacher:** A variable records a characteristic that can take different values across observational units. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P012 - variable]]

### Turn 4: Categorical variable
**Learner:** How should I understand categorical variable?
**Teacher:** A categorical variable assigns observations to groups or labels; arithmetic on its labels is usually meaningless. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P013 - categorical-variable]]

### Turn 5: Quantitative variable
**Learner:** How should I understand quantitative variable?
**Teacher:** A quantitative variable represents a count or measurement for which numerical differences have meaning. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P014 - quantitative-variable]]

### Turn 6: Discrete variable
**Learner:** How should I understand discrete variable?
**Teacher:** A discrete quantitative variable has a countable set of possible values, often whole-number counts. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P015 - discrete-variable]]

### Turn 7: Continuous variable
**Learner:** How should I understand continuous variable?
**Teacher:** A continuous measurement can in principle take any value in an interval, subject to measurement precision. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P016 - continuous-variable]]

### Turn 8: Measurement scale
**Learner:** How should I understand measurement scale?
**Teacher:** Nominal, ordinal, interval, and ratio scales encode increasing structure; the scale limits meaningful comparisons. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P017 - measurement-scale]]

### Turn 9: Operational definition
**Learner:** How should I understand operational definition?
**Teacher:** An operational definition states exactly how a concept is measured or classified in a study. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P018 - operational-definition]]

### Turn 10: Data dictionary
**Learner:** How should I understand data dictionary?
**Teacher:** A data dictionary documents variable names, meanings, units, allowed values, and missing-value conventions. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P019 - data-dictionary]]
