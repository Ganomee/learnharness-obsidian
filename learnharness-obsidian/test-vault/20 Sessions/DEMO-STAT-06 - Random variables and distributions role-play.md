## Dialogue

### Turn 1: Random variable
**Learner:** How should I understand random variable?
**Teacher:** A random variable assigns a numerical value to each outcome of a random process. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P060 - random-variable]]

### Turn 2: Probability distribution
**Learner:** How should I understand probability distribution?
**Teacher:** A probability distribution assigns probabilities to possible values or ranges and must total one. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P061 - probability-distribution]]

### Turn 3: Expected value
**Learner:** How should I understand expected value?
**Teacher:** The expected value is the probability-weighted average of outcomes over repeated trials; it need not be an attainable outcome. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P062 - expected-value]]

### Turn 4: Linearity of expectation
**Learner:** How should I understand linearity of expectation?
**Teacher:** The expected value of a sum equals the sum of expected values when those expectations exist, without requiring independence. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P063 - linearity-of-expectation]]

### Turn 5: Variance of a sum
**Learner:** How should I understand variance of a sum?
**Teacher:** For two variables, variance of their sum includes twice their covariance; variances simply add only when covariance is zero. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P064 - variance-of-a-sum]]

### Turn 6: Bernoulli trial
**Learner:** How should I understand bernoulli trial?
**Teacher:** A Bernoulli variable records one binary outcome with success probability p and failure probability 1-p. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P065 - bernoulli-trial]]

### Turn 7: Binomial model
**Learner:** How should I understand binomial model?
**Teacher:** A binomial count models successes in a fixed number of independent Bernoulli trials with a common success probability. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P066 - binomial-model]]

### Turn 8: Normal distribution
**Learner:** How should I understand normal distribution?
**Teacher:** A normal distribution is a symmetric, bell-shaped continuous model defined by its mean and standard deviation. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P067 - normal-distribution]]

### Turn 9: Standard normal
**Learner:** How should I understand standard normal?
**Teacher:** The standard normal distribution has mean zero and standard deviation one. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P068 - standard-normal]]

### Turn 10: Law of large numbers
**Learner:** How should I understand law of large numbers?
**Teacher:** Under suitable conditions, an average of repeated observations approaches its expected value as the number of observations grows. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P069 - law-of-large-numbers]]
