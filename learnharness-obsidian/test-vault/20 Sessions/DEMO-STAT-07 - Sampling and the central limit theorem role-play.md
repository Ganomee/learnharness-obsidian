## Dialogue

### Turn 1: Sampling distribution
**Learner:** How should I understand sampling distribution?
**Teacher:** A sampling distribution describes how a statistic varies across repeated samples under a specified design. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P070 - sampling-distribution]]

### Turn 2: Standard error
**Learner:** How should I understand standard error?
**Teacher:** A standard error is the standard deviation of a statistic’s sampling distribution, often estimated from sample data. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P071 - standard-error]]

### Turn 3: Sample mean standard error
**Learner:** How should I understand sample mean standard error?
**Teacher:** For independent observations with finite variance, the sample mean’s standard error is population SD divided by square root of n. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P072 - sample-mean-standard-error]]

### Turn 4: Central limit theorem
**Learner:** How should I understand central limit theorem?
**Teacher:** Under common regularity conditions, standardized sums or means become approximately normal as sample size grows. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P073 - central-limit-theorem]]

### Turn 5: Sample size effect
**Learner:** How should I understand sample size effect?
**Teacher:** Larger independent samples usually reduce standard errors at a rate proportional to one over square root of sample size. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P074 - sample-size-effect]]

### Turn 6: Finite population correction
**Learner:** How should I understand finite population correction?
**Teacher:** Sampling a substantial fraction without replacement from a finite population can reduce variance relative to an infinite-population approximation. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P075 - finite-population-correction]]

### Turn 7: Stratified sampling
**Learner:** How should I understand stratified sampling?
**Teacher:** Stratified sampling samples within defined subgroups and can improve precision when allocation and analysis reflect the design. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P076 - stratified-sampling]]

### Turn 8: Cluster sampling
**Learner:** How should I understand cluster sampling?
**Teacher:** Cluster sampling selects groups first; similarity within clusters can make the effective information smaller than the raw count suggests. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P077 - cluster-sampling]]

### Turn 9: Bootstrap
**Learner:** How should I understand bootstrap?
**Teacher:** The bootstrap approximates a statistic’s sampling variability by resampling observed units with replacement, subject to design assumptions. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P078 - bootstrap]]

### Turn 10: Permutation test
**Learner:** How should I understand permutation test?
**Teacher:** A permutation test compares a statistic to values generated by rearrangements allowed under a null exchangeability assumption. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P079 - permutation-test]]
