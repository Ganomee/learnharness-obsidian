## Dialogue

### Turn 1: Observational study
**Learner:** How should I understand observational study?
**Teacher:** An observational study records exposure or conditions without assigning them to participants. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P020 - observational-study]]

### Turn 2: Experiment
**Learner:** How should I understand experiment?
**Teacher:** An experiment assigns a treatment or condition and measures outcomes under a defined protocol. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P021 - experiment]]

### Turn 3: Random assignment
**Learner:** How should I understand random assignment?
**Teacher:** Random assignment uses chance to allocate experimental units to conditions, helping balance confounders on average. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P022 - random-assignment]]

### Turn 4: Random sampling
**Learner:** How should I understand random sampling?
**Teacher:** Random sampling uses chance to select units from a target population, supporting population generalization when well executed. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P023 - random-sampling]]

### Turn 5: Confounder
**Learner:** How should I understand confounder?
**Teacher:** A confounder is associated with both an exposure and an outcome and can distort their observed relationship. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P024 - confounder]]

### Turn 6: Selection bias
**Learner:** How should I understand selection bias?
**Teacher:** Selection bias occurs when inclusion or participation is related to variables relevant to the estimate. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P025 - selection-bias]]

### Turn 7: Nonresponse bias
**Learner:** How should I understand nonresponse bias?
**Teacher:** Nonresponse bias can arise when respondents differ systematically from nonrespondents on the outcome of interest. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P026 - nonresponse-bias]]

### Turn 8: Measurement bias
**Learner:** How should I understand measurement bias?
**Teacher:** Measurement bias is systematic error caused by an instrument, procedure, or respondent behavior. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P027 - measurement-bias]]

### Turn 9: Blinding
**Learner:** How should I understand blinding?
**Teacher:** Blinding masks treatment assignment from specified people to reduce expectation-driven behavior or measurement. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P028 - blinding]]

### Turn 10: Causal inference
**Learner:** How should I understand causal inference?
**Teacher:** Causal conclusions require a design and assumptions that address alternative explanations, not merely an observed association. The linked note records the concept and its conditions: [[40 Knowledge/P029 - causal-inference]]
